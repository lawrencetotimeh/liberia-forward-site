Liberia Forward Registered and operating in Liberia

We work where the gap is: classrooms, archives, and farms.

Liberia Forward runs programs in civic education, historical education, and community agriculture. The work is carried out on the ground in Liberia by a small team, supported by a network of collaborators, advisors, and partners rather than a large permanent staff.

Margibi County

Black soldier fly larvae production

Liberia is working to produce its own eggs and fish while importing the protein that feeds them. That gap is where we are putting our agricultural work.

Poultry farmers in Liberia have reported paying between US$100 and US$120 per bag for imported feed, with limited local access to corn, the main feed ingredient. For fish farmers, feed can account for close to half of operating expenses. Over the same period the price of a carton of eggs in Monrovia has climbed from roughly US$50 to US$70, and domestic production has reportedly declined.

Black soldier fly larvae contain roughly 40 to 50 percent protein, with an amino acid profile comparable to fishmeal, and they are raised on organic waste that communities already discard. Research across poultry and tilapia feeding trials has found larvae meal works as a partial substitute for fishmeal while lowering both feed cost and overall cost of production.

This is becoming our flagship agricultural program because it addresses the input, not the output. Eggs and fish are two of the most important proteins in the Liberian diet, and neither can be produced at scale on feed that has to be bought abroad.

Animals cannot be raised without feed. We treat feed as the first problem, not the last one.

Grand Cape Mount County

Community agriculture

We work directly with two communities in Grand Cape Mount on food production under local management. Our project manager lives and works on site, and production is reported weekly.

Our approach is to keep operations small enough to be run honestly and measured accurately. We would rather report real numbers from one village than estimates from ten.

Liberia

Civic education

Most Liberians hold rights under law that no one has ever shown them how to use. Public records they are entitled to request. Reports the government is required to publish. Revenue their own county is owed.

We teach what those rights are and how to exercise them, in plain language, in the communities where we already work.

Archival and public history

Historical education

We work with Dr. Carl Patrick Burrowes, a Liberian historian, to bring his scholarship to a wider audience.

A great deal of Liberia's documented history sits in academic work that never reaches the people it belongs to. Our role is to help carry it across that distance, in formats Liberians at home and in the diaspora will actually read and share.
